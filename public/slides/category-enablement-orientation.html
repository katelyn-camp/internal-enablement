The old playbook doesn't fit

Marketing has always worked in channels, each with a clear owner. AI search fell under SEO by default, but it actually spans a lot more channels than that, and nobody owns all of them yet.

AI Search vs. AI Discovery

A buyer asks an AI tool: "What's the difference between Visa and Mastercard, and which is better for travel?"

AI Search

AI explains the differences, the buyer evaluates the options, clicks through to learn more, and may apply for a card. The buyer asked for this comparison directly.

AI Discovery

In that same answer, AI also brings up American Express as another option. Amex just entered the buyer's consideration set, even though the buyer never asked about it.

AI Discovery is the process of earning a place in that consideration set, through paid and earned channels, before the AI ever finishes the answer.

How we actually say this out loud

"AI search" is what buyers already say, so that's the term to use in a sales conversation, it matches where their head is at today. "AI discovery" shows up more on the website and in thought leadership, where the goal is to widen how the market thinks about this beyond just their own site.

What Owned Content is

Formerly known as on-site
What it is

Content and structureDefinitionHow a site is organized and marked up: headings, page hierarchy, schema, internal links, not just the words on the page.ExampleClear headings and structured data so a page's topic is obvious, instead of one long wall of text. on a brand's own domain: the material an AI system can crawl, check facts against, and the page a visitor lands on when a citation elsewhere sends them your way.

Why it matters to AI search

It's the verification layer, not the discovery layer. Most AI citations come from third-party sources, not brand sites; owned content matters because it's what an AI system checks a third-party claim about you against.

Why it matters to the business

If your own site is thinDefinitionContent that exists, but doesn't say much, restated in different words without much real depth.Example32 nearly-identical pages, one per city, that differ only by the city name., outdated, or inconsistent, it doesn't just make your site invisible; it undermines the third-party mentions that actually drive citations. Entity detailsDefinitionThe basic facts that identify who you are: your name, what you do, and key facts about your business.ExampleBeing called "Acme Corp" on one page and just "Acme" on another confuses the picture. need to match everywhere, and your site is the one place you fully control.

Who benefits most

Companies in a complex or unfamiliar categoryDefinitionA market or product that needs real explanation before a buyer can even compare options.ExampleA new type of financial product, versus something everyone already understands, like a t-shirt., where a wrong or missing fact on your own site risks propagating into third-party coverage and AI answers about you.

Owned Content

The most accurate, up-to-date source of truth about you, regardless of whether it's getting cited.

Good fit

B2B; B2C high-considerationDefinitionA consumer purchase big or important enough that people research before buying.ExampleChoosing a mattress or a mortgage lender, not grabbing a candy bar at checkout.; established, crawlable domainDefinitionA website that's been around a while and technically lets AI/search bots read its pages.ExampleA company's main site, indexed and readable, as opposed to a brand-new site or one blocking bots..

Not a fit

Low-considerationDefinitionA purchase people make quickly, without much research, usually cheap or habitual.ExampleBuying a snack, a phone case, or a t-shirt. B2C / CPGDefinitionConsumer Packaged Goods: everyday products people buy often and replace regularly.ExampleShampoo, cereal, laundry detergent. / DTCDefinitionDirect-to-Consumer: a brand that sells straight to shoppers instead of through a retailer.ExampleA mattress brand that only sells on its own website, not in stores.; locked CMSDefinitionA website content system the company doesn't control or can't easily edit.ExampleAn old site built on outdated software that only one outside contractor can update..

What's measured
  • Crawl and index coverageDefinitionWhether AI/search bots can actually read a site's pages and have them stored to reference later.ExampleA page blocked in robots.txt won't show up here, even if a human can see it just fine.
  • Facts about you staying consistent everywhere AI can find them
  • Error rate: how often AI answers about you contradict what your own site says
When this is the right lever
  • Always, alongside earned-media and community work, not instead of it
  • Especially when an audit finds your site out of sync with what's said elsewhere
  • Or when AI answers about you are getting facts wrong

What External Content is

Formerly known as off-site
What it is

Third-party content a brand doesn't own or control: "best of" listiclesDefinition"Best of" or ranked list-style articles that group and compare options in one place.Example"10 Best Project Management Tools for Small Teams." and roundups, review platforms, and PR/press coverage — distinct from the social/influencer and community channels covered later.

Why it matters to AI search

This is where other people back up what you say about yourself, at every stage, not just near the purchase decisionDefinitionThe moment a buyer picks a specific option and commits to it.ExampleChoosing Brand A over Brand B and adding it to cart.. Independent sources get trusted more than a brand's own claims about itself whether someone is learning the category exists or comparing named vendors.

Why it matters to the business

It's the channel most platforms underinvest in, because it's easy to mistake for a late-funnel tacticDefinitionSomething done right before a buyer decides, not something that shapes earlier stages.ExampleA comparison page shown to someone about to buy; not the content that helps them learn the category exists in the first place.. In practice it carries weight from first discovery through final comparison, and thin coverageDefinitionBeing mentioned in very few outside places, or only briefly and shallowly.ExampleOne outdated article name-drops you, but no reviews, comparisons, or recent press exist. here shows up as an AI-visibility gap at every stage, not just at decision time.

Who benefits most

Any brand in a category people actively write "best of," review, or comparison content about, which covers most B2B and established B2C. Usually the most under-resourced channel relative to how much it actually drives citations.

What it is

Paid placementDefinitionPaying to appear in an AI answer, rather than being included because the AI found you on its own.ExampleA sponsored mention inside a ChatGPT response, similar to a paid search ad. inside AI answer surfaces. Why it matters to AI search

It buys speed and control in categories where the decision is fast and not research-heavy. It's also a quick, low-commitment way to test whether a topic is worth building organic content around, whether or not the purchase decision itself is fast.

What Social & Influencer is

What it is

OwnedDefinitionContent posted from a brand's own official social accounts, fully controlled by the brand.ExampleA company's own LinkedIn page posting an update., earnedDefinitionMentions or posts about a brand that the brand didn't create or pay for.ExampleA customer posting a positive review on their own account., and creator-partnershipDefinitionPaid collaboration with an individual creator to make content about a brand.ExamplePaying a YouTuber to review a product in their own video. presence on social platforms. LinkedIn and YouTube carry outsized weight for professional and considered-purchaseDefinitionA purchase a buyer thinks carefully about and compares options for, rather than deciding on impulse.ExampleEvaluating several project management tools before choosing one. content; broader platforms plus paid creator content matter more for everyday consumer categoriesDefinitionCommon, frequently-bought consumer products where the decision doesn't take much thought.ExampleSnacks, phone cases, basic clothing.. AI systems read all of it as a signal of activity and relevance.

Why it matters to AI search

Individual and creator voices get cited more than brand pages on the same platforms, because AI systems favor content that reads as independent testimony. That favors organic creator content and influencer content that reads authentically; heavily disclosed, ad-like sponsored content doesn't carry the same weight.

Why it matters to the business

It builds the direct audience relationshipDefinitionAn owned connection with your audience that doesn't depend on an algorithm or another platform to reach them.ExampleFollowers who recognize your brand directly, versus relying entirely on an article that happens to mention you. every other channel can point back to, and extends it through creators whose voice carries more independent weight than the brand's own.

Who benefits most

Any brand with genuine presence on the platforms AI models actually pull from, most reliably LinkedIn and YouTube, and only where the content reads as independent rather than obviously sponsored. Less a B2B/B2C checkbox than a question of which platforms carry citation weight in your category.

Most social/influencer teams today are stuck measuring this with shallow numbers, follower count, reach, engagement. We're not replacing those. We're adding something nobody else has: visibility into whether that content is actually showing up and getting cited in AI answers.

When this is the right lever
  • Consideration stage for B2C
  • Ongoing presence-building for B2B self-serve products

What Community is

What it is

Unmoderated third-party discussion: Reddit, Quora, review threads, where real customers talk about a category with no brand involvement.

Why it matters to AI search

It's the most trusted source there is for backing up (or contradicting) what a brand says about itself. Unprompted, unbranded conversation is exactly what "independent sources trusted more than a brand's own claims" looks like in practice.

Why it matters to the business

It's the closest thing to word-of-mouth at internet scale, and it's very hard to manufacture after the fact.

Who benefits most

Any brand in a category with existing active discussion, regardless of B2B or B2C. What matters is discussion volume, not company type.

As an example: Reddit fits into what Community as a channel looks like. As we've seen, ChatGPT can all of a sudden stop citing Reddit as frequently as it was before.

Community

Good fit

B2C; B2B self-serve products; categories with active Reddit / Quora / review discussion.

Not a fit

No existing discussion volume; brands that can't tolerate unmoderated surfaces.

What's measured
  • Mention volume
  • Sentiment
  • Share of discussion relative to competitorsDefinitionHow much of the conversation in a category is about you versus how much is about other brands people mention alongside you.ExampleOut of 100 Reddit threads about a category, your brand comes up in 20 and a competitor comes up in 60.
When this is the right lever
  • Across the funnel, not just near the decision
  • Works the same way External does, independent voices backing up your story
  • Carries weight from early discussion through final comparison, usually alongside External
